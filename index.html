01 / Research brief

The common scientific frame for both collaborators.

Working hypothesis
CENTRAL QUESTION
Does the relationship between night shift work and breast cancer history vary with sleep duration and metabolic health, and do ML models detect additional predictive patterns?

The aim is to distinguish interesting patterns from artifacts of confounding, selection and data limitations. A null or inconclusive result is still informative.

Occupational shift workSleep durationBMI & metabolic factorsBreast cancer history

Guardrails

NHANES is cross-sectional. Current work schedule may follow cancer diagnosis. Work schedule is not a direct measure of light-at-night or melatonin. This MVP does not estimate personal clinical risk and cannot support causal claims.

Research questions

H1 · Exposure associationIs work schedule associated with breast cancer history after prespecified adjustment?
H2 · Sleep interactionDoes this association differ by reported sleep duration?
H3 · Predictive valueDo shift work and sleep improve predictive performance over baseline covariates?

Methods

Survey-weighted descriptive analysis and regression; carefully separated exploratory ML experiments; missing-data analysis; attention to case counts and uncertainty.

03 / Four-week roadmap

Milestones rather than rigid dates. Adjust the schedule as you learn.

WEEK 01 · DISCOVERY

Is it feasible?

Review literature, merge public data, inspect missingness and count eligible breast cancer cases.

WEEK 02 · INFERENCE

What is associated?

Plan confounder adjustment, examine prevalence and uncertainty with survey design in mind.

WEEK 03 · MODELLING

Can ML add value?

Compare interpretable baselines with nonlinear models only if sample size supports them.

WEEK 04 · COMMUNICATE

What did we learn?

Build demo, document limitations and prepare a short research report.
